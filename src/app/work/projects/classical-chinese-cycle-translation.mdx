## What it does

Two generation tasks over Chinese text: bidirectional translation between Classical Chinese and the
modern vernacular, and rewriting modern prose in Lu Xun's style. The translation half borrows cycle
consistency from CycleGAN — a sentence is translated, then translated back, and the round trip has
to land where it started. The goal isn't a higher one-way BLEU; it's that meaning, structure and
key facts survive both passes, which is exactly where classical–modern translation tends to drop
information or invent it.

## How it works

Discrete sampling isn't differentiable, so a cycle loss can't be backpropagated through a decoded
sentence. Instead the first translation's softmax distribution is multiplied by the embedding
matrix to form a continuous soft-token representation, which is fed to the second pass — the total
objective is translation loss plus `lambda_cycle` times the round-trip loss.

That weight turns out to matter. At `lambda_cycle=0.1`, BLEU, BERTScore and hallucination rate are
all at their most stable; pushing it higher makes the round trip more reversible but nudges the
model toward copy collapse, where it learns to echo the input rather than translate it. Evaluation
covers round-trip consistency, copy rate, and hallucination of numbers and content characters
alongside the usual translation metrics.

Built on Randeng-BART-139M for the seq2seq models and a Qwen3-4B LoRA for style transfer, trained
on the NiuTrans Classical-Modern parallel corpus. The demo runs as a Streamlit front end against a
FastAPI inference backend on a GPU server.
